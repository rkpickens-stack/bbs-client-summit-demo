Furnace not heating? Simple checks to try before you call

The first cold mornings in Hickory, Newton and Conover are when many furnaces get switched on for the first time since spring. If yours is blowing cold air or not coming on at all, a few simple checks can sometimes solve the problem in minutes. Others tell you it is time to call a technician. Here is what to look at, in the order we would check it ourselves.

Start with the thermostat

It sounds obvious, but the thermostat causes more "no heat" calls than almost anything else.

  • Make sure it is set to Heat, not Cool or Off, and that the set temperature is a few degrees above the room temperature.
  • If the fan is set to On instead of Auto, the blower will run all the time and push out cool air between heating cycles. Switch it to Auto.
  • If the screen is blank or dim, replace the batteries. Many thermostats keep working on weak batteries until the first real cold snap.
  • If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, check that a schedule or away mode is not holding the temperature down.

Give the system a few minutes after any change. Most furnaces wait briefly before the burners light and the blower starts.

Check the power and the switch

A furnace needs electricity even if it burns gas.

  • Look for a light switch on or near the furnace. It is easy to mistake for a regular light switch and turn off by accident. It should be on.
  • Check your electrical panel for a tripped breaker labeled furnace, heat or air handler. If it has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call, because that points to an electrical fault.
  • Make sure the furnace door or panel is fully closed. Most units have a safety switch that keeps the system off when the panel is loose.

Look at the air filter

A clogged filter is one of the most common reasons a furnace runs but the house stays cold. When air cannot move through the filter, the furnace can overheat and shut itself off as a safety measure, then try again later.

Pull the filter out and hold it up to a light. If you cannot see light through it, replace it. If you have pets, or there has been dust from recent work in the house, filters fill up faster than the package suggests. Note the size printed on the frame so you buy the right one.

Make sure air can get out

Walk around the house and check the supply vents and return grilles. Furniture, rugs and curtains often end up covering vents over the summer. Closing vents in unused rooms can also upset the airflow the system was designed for. Open them up and see whether the rooms warm more evenly.

Signs you should stop and call

Some problems are not do-it-yourself jobs, and a few are safety issues. Call a licensed technician if you notice any of these:

  • A smell of gas, or a rotten-egg odor. Leave the house first and call your gas utility from outside.
  • Your carbon monoxide alarm sounds.
  • The breaker trips again after you reset it.
  • You hear loud banging, grinding or screeching when the furnace starts.
  • The furnace turns on and off every few minutes without warming the house.
  • Water is pooling around the unit.
  • You have tried the checks above and still have no heat.

Preventing the next cold-morning surprise

Most of the issues above are easier to catch before winter than in the middle of a cold night. A fall tune-up lets a technician clean the burners, test the safety controls, check the flame sensor and the blower, and spot worn parts while there is still time to plan. It is also a good moment to ask questions about how your system is running.
