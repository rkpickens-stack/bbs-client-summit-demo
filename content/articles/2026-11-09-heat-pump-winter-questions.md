Heat pumps are common in homes around Hickory, Lenoir and Morganton, and they work differently from a furnace. That difference leads to a lot of good questions once the weather turns cold. Here are the ones we hear most often each winter, with straight answers.

## Is it normal for steam to come off the outdoor unit?

Usually, yes. In cold, damp weather, frost builds up on the outdoor coil. Your heat pump clears it by running a short defrost cycle, which warms the coil and melts the frost. During defrost you may see steam or a cloud of vapor rising from the unit, and you may hear a whoosh as the system switches modes. The fan on top often stops for a few minutes, then starts again.

That is all normal. What is not normal is a unit that stays covered in thick ice for hours, or ice that keeps growing on the top and sides. That can point to a defrost problem, low refrigerant or a fan issue, and it is worth having checked.

## Why does the air from my vents feel only lukewarm?

A heat pump delivers a steady stream of warm air rather than the short blasts of hot air you may remember from a furnace. If you hold your hand to a vent, the air can feel cooler than your hand even though it is warmer than the room. Over a full cycle it keeps the house comfortable.

The air should still feel noticeably warm, though. If it feels like room temperature or cold for long stretches, check that the thermostat is set to Heat, that the filter is clean and that nothing is blocking the outdoor unit. If those look fine, call for a service check.

## What is emergency heat, and should I use it?

Most heat pumps have backup heat, usually electric heat strips inside the indoor unit. The system turns them on by itself when it needs extra help, such as on the coldest mornings or during defrost.

The "Emergency Heat" or "EM Heat" setting on your thermostat forces the system to use only the backup heat. It is meant for when the outdoor unit has failed, not for everyday use, because backup heat costs more to run. If you find you need emergency heat to stay warm, that is a sign the heat pump itself needs attention.

## Should I turn the temperature way down at night?

Large setbacks often work against a heat pump. When you raise the temperature a lot in the morning, the system may lean on its more expensive backup heat to catch up. Many homeowners find a small setback, or a steady setting, more comfortable and easier on the system. Smart thermostats made for heat pumps can manage this for you.

## How can I keep the outdoor unit working well?

- Keep leaves, pine straw and mulch at least a couple of feet away from all sides.
- Do not cover the unit with a tarp in winter. It needs airflow to heat your home.
- After snow or an ice storm, gently clear snow away from the base and top. Do not chip at ice on the coil.
- Make sure gutters are not dripping water straight onto the unit, which can freeze into a solid block.

## When to call for service

Call a licensed technician if the outdoor unit stays iced over, if you hear grinding or rattling, if the system runs constantly without keeping up, or if your backup heat seems to be running all the time. A winter check can often catch a small problem before it becomes a no-heat call on a cold night.
